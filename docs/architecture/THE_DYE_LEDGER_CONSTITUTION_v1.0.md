The Dye Ledger Constitution Version 1.0 Preamble The Dye Ledger exists to make keeping score and preserving the memories, stories, and traditions of golf effortless. This Constitution defines the enduring architectural principles that govern the product. Features, workflows, and implementations may evolve, but these principles establish the long-term foundation of the platform. Chapter I â€” Identity &amp; Ownership Principle 1 â€” Ownership Users own their libraries. Rounds own their stories. Users own their identity, devices, preferences, golfer libraries, course libraries, cloud accounts, and personal settings. Rounds own participants, scores, competitions, settlements, memories, recaps, and historical facts. Principle 2 â€” Round Roles Every Round has explicit access roles. Owner Participant Viewer Owners administer the Round. Participants permanently retain historical participation. Viewers have read-only access. Principle 3 â€” Canonical Golfer Identity Every primary Dye Ledger user has an authenticated account. Every golfer has a permanent canonical Golfer Identity whether claimed or unclaimed. Verified phone numbers and verified email addresses may be used to claim an identity, but neither phone nor email is itself the identity. Historical participation exists independently of identity verification. Principle 4 â€” Personal Golfer Library Every user maintains a personal golfer library linked to canonical golfer identities. Personal preferences belong to the user. Canonical identity belongs to the golfer. Principle 5 â€” Administrative Ownership Every Round has one administrative owner. Participants permanently retain access to their historical participation. Administrative authority and historical participation are intentionally distinct. Chapter II â€” Historical Record Principle 6 â€” RoundRecord The RoundRecord is the permanent authoritative record of a Round. Devices are working copies. The cloud stores authoritative RoundRecords while preserving the offline-first architecture. Principle 7 â€” Historical Facts Rounds own historical facts. Golfers own lifetime identity. Career statistics and historical analytics are derived rather than duplicated. Principle 8 â€” Information Classes Every piece of information belongs to one of three classes: Immutable Historical Facts Living Identity Derived Analytics Each class follows its own lifecycle. Principle 9 â€” Shared Memories Shared memories become part of the Round Story while preserving contributor rights, visibility controls, moderation, correction, and privacy protections. Generated artifacts belong to the RoundRecord while remaining subject to correction and privacy protections. Principle 10 â€” Versioned History Completed RoundRecords are never silently overwritten. Corrections produce: a new version, attribution, reason, and a permanent audit trail. Prior versions remain preserved. Chapter III â€” Governance Principle 11 â€” Authorized Amendments Only authorized Round administrators may amend completed RoundRecords. Participants may propose corrections. Accepted amendments are: versioned, attributed, classified by impact, and audited. Principle 12 â€” Amendment Sessions Completed Rounds are never reopened. Corrections occur through Amendment Sessions. Publishing an Amendment Session creates a new RoundRecord version while preserving all prior versions. Chapter IV â€” Core Domain Model Principle 13 â€” The Round A Round is the permanent record of a single golf experience. The scorecard is its competitive foundation. Participants, competitions, settlements, memories, stories, and historical artifacts preserve the complete experience. Trips, tournaments, leagues, and Ryder Cups consist of multiple Rounds rather than extending the definition of a Round. Principle 14 â€” Event Occurrences An Event Occurrence is a complete first-class record. It may belong to an Event Series or exist independently. Rounds remain complete records whether or not they belong to an Event. Principle 15 â€” Competition Ownership Competitions belong to the smallest context that completely contains them. Competitions confined to a single Round belong to that Round. Competitions spanning multiple Rounds belong to the Event Occurrence that contains those Rounds. Principle 16 â€” Enduring Golfer Identity A Golfer Identity represents the enduring person rather than their current profile details. Names, phone numbers, email addresses, GHIN numbers, profile photos, and similar identifying attributes may change without creating a new Golfer Identity or altering historical facts. Historical values used during completed Rounds remain historical facts. Living profile information may evolve over time. Principle 17 â€” Event Series An Event Series represents an enduring golf tradition or recurring competitive community across time. An Event Occurrence represents one specific instance of that Series, with its own dates, participants, Rounds, competitions, settlements, memories, and historical record. Event Occurrences may exist independently and may later be associated with, moved between, or removed from an Event Series without altering their underlying historical facts. Administrative reassignment must be auditable. Principle 18 â€” Identity, Membership, Attendance &amp; Participation Identity, Membership, Attendance, and Participation are four distinct concepts. Identity represents the golfer. Membership represents a relationship to an Event Series. Attendance represents presence at an Event Occurrence. Participation represents playing in an individual Round. Each has its own lifecycle and historical significance. Principle 19 â€” Independent Lifecycles Rounds, Event Occurrences, and Event Series are independent first-class objects. Each maintains its own lifecycle and historical record. Relationships provide context but do not determine existence, status, or governance. Changes to one do not implicitly change another except where explicitly governed. Principle 20 â€” Organizational Relationships Rounds, Event Occurrences, and Event Series may be reorganized through authorized and auditable relationship changes without altering underlying historical facts. Changing organizational relationships affects context and derived analytics only. Audit history records: previous relationship, new relationship, who, when, and reason. Chapter V â€” Historical Aggregation Principle 21 â€” Event Records Each completed Event Occurrence produces a permanent Event Record. The Event Record contains occurrence-level historical facts. Event Series preserve enduring identity, membership, traditions, and governance. Series-level competitive history is derived from Event Records. Round facts remain authoritative within RoundRecords. Principle 22 â€” Competition Finality Competition results and settlements are finalized within the authoritative record that owns the competition. Single-Round competitions belong to RoundRecords. Multi-Round competitions belong to Event Records. Later Round amendments never silently rewrite finalized Event results. Material changes require: authorized review, a new Event Record version, attribution, audit, and preservation of prior versions. Chapter VI â€” Privacy &amp; Preservation Principle 23 â€” Privacy, Deletion &amp; Historical Preservation Privacy, deletion, and historical preservation are distinct operations. The following actions are independent and governed separately: removing access, hiding information from personal views, withdrawing contributed content, anonymizing living identity, archiving information, and permanent deletion. Legitimate shared historical records should be preserved whenever possible. Permanent deletion is exceptional and must be governed by explicit authorization, retention policies, legal obligations, and historical integrity. Constitutional Governance Rule Every major feature proposal shall include a Constitutional Review identifying which constitutional principles it implements, extends, or could potentially affect. If a proposal appears to conflict with an approved constitutional principle, that conflict must be identified and resolved before implementation begins. This Constitution governs the long-term evolution of The Dye Ledger and serves as the highest-level architectural authority for the product.
